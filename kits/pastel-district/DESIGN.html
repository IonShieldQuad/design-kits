Pastel District

A dreamy pink instrument panel — bokeh light behind hairline glass. Place 05 of the Li//OS desk family, and the only warm-soft place in it. The grammar is the family's and is unchanged: a 1px frame, an opaque {colors.surface} face, a sheen band along its top edge, an occlusion band along its floor, a 157° fall, a 5% tint of the accent, one drawn motif at 5%, and a hairline of accent running along the card's bottom edge.

Overview

Every other place in this family is a machined plate: a chamfer or a square corner, a lattice or an engraving cut into the face, a cold hue. Pastel District is the same instrument with a different subject — the object behind the glass is not a grid, it is nine discs of light that have gone out of focus. Three decisions follow from that and nothing else does:

  • The card is a pane over a bloom, not a faceplate. The nine discs sit inside the material, behind the tint and under the sheen, so they read as something behind the surface rather than a pattern printed on it. That is also why they are the only motif in the family that is not tiled: bokeh has no pitch to repeat at, so one stack of nine fills any box and every card gets its own constellation.
  • The frame stays one pixel. A hairline is what makes a bloom look like it is behind something. A heavier border turns the glass into a lid.
  • The geometry is round and small. {rounded.sm} / {rounded.md} / {rounded.lg} are 4 / 6 / 8px — the desk's own --rad: 6px, stepped — with no --clip and no --cut. A chamfer is a cut, and nothing in this place is cut.

The one thing the kit does not add is a glow the desk does not have. Depth here is the desk's own drop shadow; the light is the bloom already in the ground and in the nine discs, and the accent appears as a 14px bloom at 30% only where the desk put it (a primary button).

Colors

{colors.primary} is the place's #FF8FC4 — the action colour, a primary button, a link, an active nav item. It is a light fill, so {colors.on-primary} is the dark plum ink #2A0F1B at 8.4:1, not white.

{colors.tertiary} is the same pink reserved for text: the desk already solved "accent as a label" with its own --accHi, so small type takes #FFB9DB (7.6:1 on the pane) rather than the fill colour. {colors.primary-soft} is an 18% tint of the accent, used for accent badges, the active nav item and the secondary button's hover.

{colors.secondary} is the pale mint in the place's own scene, and it does the counterpoint's job: the media plate, a chart series, the mint glint arriving in one corner. It is never an action colour and it never carries small text on anything lighter than {colors.surface-2}.

{colors.neutral} is the ground. {colors.scene} is the plum field that sits over it and {colors.scene-floor} is what the ground ramps to at its base — all three are the place's own values and none of them is a preference.

The status ladder is derived, because the desk has one alert red and no ladder: mint {colors.ok}, amber {colors.warn}, red {colors.danger}, pale violet {colors.info}. See the note on {colors.danger} below — it is the one pair in this kit that needed judgement.

Typography

Chakra Petch for both voices and JetBrains Mono for labels — the family's pairing, shared by all six places, because these are six rooms in one operating system and the type is the OS. Labels are uppercase mono at {typography.label} with .14em tracking: an instrument that names its own channels, not a poster.

The pairing costs a little softness. Chakra Petch is a squared technical face and a stricter reading of "dreamy" would reach for something rounder; the family's argument is that the place supplies the register and the OS supplies the voice, and a kit that changed the type would stop being a room in the same building. Use the weight and the tracking for warmth, not a third family.

Layout

The page is a lit surface, so the ground is never blank: two soft fields of the place's plum over a 120px technical grid, the nine discs at half their card alpha, and a ramp into {colors.scene-floor} at the base. All of it is sized in %, never px — the gallery renders --bg into a ~118px tile, and a px-anchored scene would show that tile its top edge only.

The cards sit on that field with the accent hairline along their floor, and the layout should leave room under a card for it: the identity edge is at the very bottom inside the 9% occlusion band, and a card with a negative margin or a clipped overflow loses it.

Elevation & Depth

Depth is an occlusion, not a light. --shadow-1 is the desk's 0 6px 16px rgba(0,0,0,.50) and --shadow-2 its well shadow at .55; the card is already lit from behind, so the shadow's job is to say the pane is in front of the bloom. --glow is the desk's own 14px accent bloom at 30%, and it appears on a primary button only.

--blur: none. Every surface in this kit is opaque — the material's last layer is the face itself — and a blurred opaque card is a repaint cost pretending to be glass. The translucency the kit has is inside the material (the 5% tint over the discs), which no backdrop-filter can produce.

The sheen is the top 9% of the card and the occlusion its bottom 9%, both with the desk's own stops. They are what make the pane read as a sheet with an edge rather than a rounded rectangle.

Shapes

4 / 6 / 8px and a 999px pill, --cut: 0px, no --clip. The desk's --rad is 6px and the steps are small on purpose: at a 16px chamfer the nine discs would be cropped by the diagonal and the bloom would read as a badge. Native checkboxes and radios take {rounded.sm} (--check-radius), because a squared control in this place is a different kit.

Components

  • button-primary — an opaque {colors.primary} plate with a {colors.on-primary} label and the --glow bloom. It is the only filled, opaque thing in the kit, and that is what keeps it separable from the mint {colors.ok} and the red {colors.danger}.
  • button-secondary — transparent with a {colors.tertiary} label and a {colors.border-strong} hairline; hovering tints the plate with {colors.primary-soft}.
  • card — the pane: sheen, occlusion, nine discs, identity edge, 5% tint, 157° fall, and the face. One token, --pastel-district-pane, because a fill plus a border is not this material.
  • card-elevated — the nested tier on {colors.surface-2}: no discs and no identity edge, a shallow sheen and half the tint.
  • input — {colors.surface-2} at {rounded.md}, {colors.border} hairline, {colors.text} ink. Focus takes the accent hairline and a 3px {colors.primary-soft} ring.
  • badge — mono, uppercase, pill. badge-accent carries the pink tint and the accent ink; the four status badges carry a status hue on {colors.surface-2}.
  • avatar / chart-1..3 / media — where the two accents are allowed to appear as colour rather than as chrome.

Do's and Don'ts

Do

  • Keep the nine discs at the alpha they ship with. They are baked at the desk's own 5%; raising them "until they show" turns light out of focus into a dotted fill.
  • Grade any new ink against the composited pane — the lightest pixel under a disc, not #23141D.
  • Keep the frame at 1px and the radius at 6px. The softness is in the light, not in the geometry.
  • Let the identity edge have its pixel: nothing should sit flush against the bottom of a card.

Don't

  • Don't set --blur to anything but none. It would blur an opaque surface and cost a compositor layer for no visible change.
  • Don't add a glow. The bloom is already behind the glass; a second one makes the card a light source and the kit a neon theme.
  • Don't use {colors.primary} as small text. It is legal on the pane (5.7:1) but it reads as a pressed button, and the desk's own {colors.tertiary} buys 7.6:1 for the same hue.
  • Don't swap the ground for a light one and call it a variant: the pink is graded against a deep plum, and {colors.text-dim} on a white page is 2.9:1.
  • Don't put {colors.danger} next to {colors.primary} in the same control group without a label. They are 21° apart in hue and the red is only separable because it is darker and more saturated.
  • Don't tile the motif. Bokeh that repeats is a pattern.
