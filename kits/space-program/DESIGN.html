Space Program

Overview

Mission control: the room the mission is run from. Not the sky — outer-space owns the sky — and not the pilot's weapons HUD, which is mecha-pilot. This is the program: an engineering drawing sheet, white plates bolted onto it, and the paperwork a mission is actually flown by. A checklist, a block number, a lamp cluster, a plotted trace.

The register is documentation, and the ground is white on purpose. --surface is pure white (the wall plates), --surface-2 is a pale cool well (fields, badges, readouts), and --bg is a near-white drawing sheet with a rimmed border 14px in from the page edge, a 48px module grid and a fine 8px drafting grid. Depth is a hard cast edge, not a shadow: a card is a plate standing two pixels proud of the sheet. Ink is deep and institutional — the dark of a printed form, never a blue-grey haze.

One colour acts. Agency blue #12539f is the only thing on the page that means "do this"; the signal ladder — GO green, CAUTION amber, ABORT red — carries state and never an action. The second accent, #2b6fc4, is the plotter pen: the same family one step brighter, for charts, second series and the traced line. Nothing glows. Nothing is blurred. A control room clicks rather than fades.

Colors

Agency blue #12539f is the action: the primary key, the focus ring, the active tab, and the ink of every link and label that means "go here". Its text-safe member ships separately as --accent-ink (#0e3f8f, 8.47:1 on the pale well and 6.69:1 even on its own 13% tint composited over it) because a fill that carries a white label is not automatically a fill that reads as an 11px legend.

The second accent, #2b6fc4, is the plotter pen — the same family one step brighter. It belongs to charts, second data series and the traced line in the media panel. It is never an action, and it is deliberately not a third hue: a console room has one blue, and adding a teal "because technology" would make this kit a different, noisier one.

The signal ladder is clarity colouring — colour for legibility, not decoration. GO #10703a (5.30:1), CAUTION #8f5404 (5.24:1), ABORT #b81b12 (5.57:1) are each the brightest value their hue can take and still carry a badge label on the pale well. The lamp glass in the drawn motifs is the same hue one step brighter (#16a34a, #e08a0c, #d02014) — which is how a real lamp works: bright glass, dark legend. That split is the whole of the kit's relationship to status colour.

The ground is white and the plate is white; they are told apart by a cast edge and a 1px joint line, not by tint. Because a near-white kit is graded the other way round from a dark one, every ink is solved against the darkest ground that carries type — --surface-2 and the module-grid intersection — and never against white. See the table in README.md; the numbers there are worst-case.

Typography

Three faces, three jobs. Big Shoulders Display is the display: a condensed, sign-painter's grotesque with an engineeering-signage gait, used for headings and card titles only. IBM Plex Sans is the body — a utilitarian text face designed for technical documentation, and the reason a paragraph of this kit stays a paragraph. Overpass Mono is the readout: every kicker, legend, badge, table header, caption and metric is set in it, because on this page anything that is a fact is printed in the telemetry face. Overpass comes from highway signage, so it holds up at 12px in caps.

Caps labels run +0.14em — stencilled, but narrower than a HUD (mecha-pilot uses .16em), because this is a formed legend on a plate rather than a glow on a screen.

Layout

Spacing is documentation-tight and completely regular: 16px gutters, 24px between blocks, and a background that draws the sheet rather than decorating it — a rimmed border, a 48px module grid and an 8px drafting grid, all hard-edged. Panels align to the module grid and to each other; there is no asymmetry and no airiness, because a form is laid out by a draughtsman. Corners are machined, 2–4px, not rounded and not chamfered (carbon owns the cut corner).

Elevation & Depth

Depth is a cast edge, never a shadow. A card is a white plate standing 2px proud of the sheet on two hard rules (--shadow-1); an elevated card is 4px (--shadow-2). Fields are milled into the plate rather than laid on it (--input-inset), the navigation is a milled console channel (--bar-shadow) with the current item riding in it as a plate (--bar-item-shadow), and the switch is a seated track with a machined knob (--toggle-shadow, --toggle-knob-shadow).

Controls are analog keys. A button rests on a cast edge with a top inner highlight (--btn-shadow), and on press that edge collapses into an inset well (--btn-shadow-active) — the control visibly travels into the panel. --dur is 80ms and --ease is linear, so the transition snaps instead of easing: it clicks. Nothing in the kit is blurred: --blur: none, and there is not a single radial gradient in the file.

Shapes

The kit's geometry is three drawn devices, each doing a different job — the procedure, the identity, the state:

  • --space-program-checklist — the procedure. A blocky flight-checklist strip: a filled header bar, three ruled columns, three ticked square boxes and one open box, with value leaders struck down the right. A repeating gradient makes an even rule pattern; it cannot make a tick inside a box.
  • --space-program-blocknumber — the identity. A drawing title block: a heavy frame, a hatched revision cell, a dark binding edge, and a stencil numeral (04) drawn as exact geometry with its stencil breaks, plus a legend rule beneath it.
  • --space-program-lamps — the state. A console lamp cluster: four square lamps in dark bezels with a bright glass, a hard specular band and a leader rule, seated in a milled channel — GO · CAUTION · ABORT · OFF.

The plotted trace and the graduation strip are not a fourth tile: they live on the card media panel (--media-bg, a plotter output with a drafting grid, a two-pen trace and a numbered graduation), which is where a plot belongs.

Components

Buttons are agency-blue keys with white labels or white plates with blue labels; every variant wears the same cast edge and the same collapsed pressed state, so the whole control layer is one physical object. Cards are white plates on a cast edge; the elevated card is the pale well. Inputs are milled, with a square native checkbox drawn as a checklist box (--check-appearance: none, --check-radius: 0px). Badges are stamped plates carrying mono caps: on the pale well for the neutral one, on the sheet for the signal ones. Media panels are plotter output, opaque. Progress and the avatar are a segmented console channel (--fill-bg), hard-stopped, with both segments dark enough for the white label.

Do's and Don'ts

  • Do keep the ground white and the plates whiter. This kit's whole premise is that a console room is a bright room.
  • Do keep exactly one action colour. Blue acts; green, amber and red only report.
  • Do keep the ladder legible: a status colour is used at the brightest value that still clears 4.5:1 as a label, and lamp glass is brighter than lamp legend.
  • Do keep every control a physical key — cast edge at rest, inset well when pressed, 80ms linear.
  • Do let the checklist, the block number and the lamp cluster do the identifying work.
  • Don't add a glow, a blur or a soft shadow. There is no bloom in this kit; --glow is a machined rim on the primary key, not a halo.
  • Don't round anything above 4px or chamfer a corner — carbon owns the cut corner.
  • Don't put a starfield, a nebula or a spacecraft in it. That is outer-space, and it is the sky; this is the room.
