Overview

This kit is the ground, not the sky. It is the surface a mission is standing on: grey-brown regolith (#e6e2d7 at the top of the page settling to #d2ccbf), a near-black warm ink, one dust-covered amber for action, and a cold blue-grey that appears for exactly one reason — on the Moon the shadow really is blue, because airless shadow is lit by earthshine and starlight and nothing else. There is no violet, no neon, no glow, no starfield and no black in this palette. It is a mineral register, and the material is granular.

Two grades of one material. Regolith is drawn, not sampled, and it ships in two grades because one cannot do both of this kit's jobs:

  • --moon-rover-dust — the ground grade. Sunlit chalk grains with every darkening element capped at 11% alpha, because type sits on it. Composited from the shipped artwork, the worst pixel — every darkening layer of the texture landing on the same spot, over the darkest --bg stop — still measures 7.53:1 for {colors.text} and 4.78:1 for --text-dim.
  • --moon-rover-grit — the material grade. The same regolith with its micro-shadows kept: each grain has a lit facet and a real shaded facet and a hairline cast sliver. It is used only where no type can sit — the card media panel and the signature tiles.

That split is the kit's load-bearing decision. Regolith with honest micro-shadows is not a background you can set 12px captions on; pretending otherwise is how a kit ships as "dusty colours" with unreadable hints.

The hard sun is a geometry, not a blur. Every shadow is a zero-blur offset plate — --shadow-1/--shadow-2, --btn-shadow, --bar-shadow, --bar-item-shadow, --toggle-shadow, --toggle-knob-shadow, --glow. The media panel's shadow is a caster and its projection: a low boulder field in the artwork, and that silhouette put through a shear matrix — so the shadow is longer than the object, which is the one thing a drop shadow can never say — drawn translucent so the grain of the ground still reads inside the darkness. (The first version of that panel used a straight hard-stop gradient band: in a real 300×96 card it read as a stray dark overlay, because a shadow with no caster, no contact line and no shape is a stripe.) --blur is none; an airless body has no diffusion.

One sentence: the lunar surface and the machine standing on it — grey-brown regolith under one unfiltered sun, hard black shadow plates, drawn dust, and one dust-covered amber doing all the acting.

Colors

  • Primary (#d9821c) — the dusty amber, and it is a FILL. It fills the primary button, the checked controls, the progress bar and the avatar, and nothing else. It is the colour of hardware that has been out in the dust: saturated enough to act, dirty enough not to glow. As a label on this ground it measures 1.83:1 — a fill is not an ink.
  • Primary-hover (#eb9a33) — the dust is blown off it. The plate comes up on press, which is both the physically honest move (a sunlit surface under a hand) and the legible one: its own label rises from 6.23:1 to 8.01:1.
  • Primary-ink (#191410) — dust-black printed on the amber plate. An amber fill takes dark ink, never white (white on #d9821c is ~2.2:1).
  • Primary-text (#5e2b03) / primary-text-hover (#4a2102) — the amber as text: eyebrows, links, active nav and tab labels, secondary-button labels, inline code, .table code. A wet-rust brown of the same family: 6.22:1 on the deepest recess and 5.76:1 on its own 15% tint composited over that recess. This pair is why the palette works at all.
  • Secondary (#4a6a86) — shadow blue-grey. The one cold colour in the kit and the reason the palette is not monochrome-warm. It is the shadow side of the media panel and the second stop of fills; it never acts. Its darker family members are --info (#26405a) and --focus-ring (#2e4a63).
  • Tertiary (#26405a) — telemetry blue. Data and readouts, so an instrument never borrows the action colour. (It is --info in tokens.css.)
  • Neutral (#ded9cd) — the ground's dominant stop and the solid stand-in, since colors: cannot hold a gradient (the linter errors on one). The two ramp stops ship as colours of their own: ground-top (#e6e2d7) and ground-bottom (#d2ccbf), referenced by page and ground-lower.
  • Surface (#eeeae0) / surface-2 (#c4beb0) — and note the direction, because it is the whole light model: a card is a sunlit plate, a shade lighter than the ground (#eeeae0 against a #d2ccbf lower ground), and an inset is a scooped recess, a shade darker (#c4beb0) — the same regolith with the sun taken off it. Neither is white paper, neither is a black well.
  • Text (#1e1c17) / text-muted (#38332b) — the near-black warm ink and its second step.
  • Success (#1f4a28) / warning (#553d04) / error (#7a2714) — deep mineral inks: a dark dusty green, a deep ochre and an oxide red. All three are solved on --surface-2, the darkest ground any of them is drawn on (a badge keeps its own recess background), not on the easy pale card. --warn is deliberately an ochre rather than a dimmed amber, so a caution never reads as the action.

Deliberately not in the map above: --text-dim: #423d31 (captions, hints, table headers, the binding pair in this kit), --accent-ink-hover, --border: #a49c8a / --border-strong: #786f5d (the hard seams — the DESIGN.md component schema has no borderColor property), --accent-soft: rgba(217,130,28,.15) (a translucent tint is not a colour), --focus-ring (#2e4a63) and --accent-2 as a fill. Listing any of them would only produce orphan warnings for values that are very much in use. Exact values are in tokens.css.

Typography

A stamped-hardware voice, in two families plus an instrument mono:

  • Saira Condensed — display. A condensed technical grotesque. Hardware lettering is condensed and heavy, because it is stencilled onto a curved plate with a pantograph rather than set in a book; the condensed face is the closest honest analogue of a stamped data plate in the Google library. It sets the h1, section headings and card titles.
  • Saira — body. The same superfamily at text width, so a plate's label and a plate's note share a hand rather than merely coexisting. This is the face a paragraph is read in.
  • Fira Mono — labels, eyebrows, badges, table headers, readouts. A rover's data plate, not a HUD and not a terminal: mono is what an instrument prints in. The readout register (0.9rem, +0.01em) carries IDs, version strings and numerals so a column of figures lines up as instrumentation.
  • Caps labels are tracked +0.12em — stamped and tracked, tighter than a HUD's 0.14–0.18em, because a pressed label is compressed into metal, not spaced out on glass.
  • Upright, always. --style-display: normal: stencils do not lean. The contract names a font family, never its style, so --style-display is declared rather than left to a fallback.

There is no third face and nothing decorative: this kit's character is in the material, not in the lettering.

Layout

The shared column (min(1040px, 100% - 2.5rem)) over a dust-textured regolith ground. Section boundaries are 2px machined seams; inside a panel the rhythm is tight (8–14px) so controls group by proximity rather than by boxing. Cards are an auto-fit grid that collapses to one column on a phone.

Controls are 40px tall with 12px×20px button padding and 20px card padding. The spacing scale runs 4 · 8 · 16 · 28 · 44, and the gap between the ground band and the ground band below it is the 44px step, so the page breathes the way a wide flat surface does.

Elevation & Depth

Depth is a plate of shadow, not a haze. --shadow-1 is 4px 4px 0 0 var(--moon-rover-shade) plus a one-pixel sunlit lip (inset 1px 1px 0 rgba(255,253,246,.58)); --shadow-2 is the same at 7px 7px. The plate measures 2.2:1 against the ground — a shadow you can see, not a hint. Both are zero-blur, because a shallow sun throws a plate of shadow and that plate has an edge. There is no blurred drop shadow anywhere in this kit.

  • Every button variant wears --btn-shadow (3px 3px 0 0), not just the primary: on a surface where everything is lit from one direction, everything casts.
  • The primary plate casts further (--glow: 5px 5px), because a raised plate on a sunlit surface throws a longer shadow than a flush one. --glow is reused as the hard plate rather than a halo — there is nothing to glow here.
  • Nav and tabs are milled into the ground (--bar-shadow: inset 0 2px 0), and the current item is a plate riding in that channel (--bar-item-shadow).
  • Inputs are scooped (--input-inset): the sunward inner wall of the scoop is in shadow (dark, top-left) and the wall opposite catches the light (lit, bottom-right). That is the same light model as the cast shadows, inverted — and it is why the recess reads as a hole rather than as a raised bevel.
  • --blur: none. card-elevated uses --surface-2 and --shadow-2, so it is more recessed, not more lifted.

Shapes

Crisp plate geometry: --radius-sm: 2px, --radius-md: 3px, --radius-lg: 5px, and --radius-pill: 3px — deliberately not a capsule. Nothing on a rover is rounded, so badges, the toggle track, the switch knob, status dots and the avatar are all square-ish machined plates; that single decision does more to distinguish this kit's silhouette than any colour.

--cut: 0px and no --clip: this kit wants its 2px frame on all four edges. A chamfer would cut the border along the diagonal, eat the zero-blur cast shadows that are the kit, and force the focus ring inward — three costs for a silhouette this palette does not want.

Square native controls. --check-appearance: none with a 2px rim (--check-border), a pale plate (--check-bg: #eeeae0) and a filled amber square when checked (--check-checked). Native checkboxes ignore border-radius, so without this a kit whose only shape rule is "square" would render round radios.

Components

  • button-primary — the amber plate: dust-black label ({colors.primary-ink}) in the mono label style, 3px radius, 2px rim, and the longest hard shadow in the kit. It is a fill, never a text colour.
  • button-primary-hover — the same plate with the dust blown off ({colors.primary-hover}), still dust-black. See Colors for why the fill brightens rather than darkens.
  • button-secondary / button-ghost / button-danger — plates in the sunlit surface, the ground tone or open, taking wet-rust, muted-ink or oxide-red labels. Secondary actions are recessed or flush, never filled: only one thing in this kit is amber.
  • card / ground-lower / card-media — the sunlit plate, the ground's lower stop, and the media panel. The media panel is where the kit paints a surface rather than a colour: a boulder field and its cast shadow, regolith at two scales, opaque (--media-op: 1).
  • card-title — Saira Condensed at weight 650–700: a stencilled plate heading, read across a field.
  • input — the scooped recess ({colors.surface-2}) with a 2px machined rim; on focus the rim takes the amber and the well takes a 3px --accent-soft halo.
  • badge / badge-accent / badge-ok / badge-warn / badge-danger — 3px-radius stamped plates in the recess, with the amber tint carrying the accent label and the deep mineral inks carrying status. None of them is a capsule.
  • table-header — mono, uppercase, +0.12em, muted: the engraved column legend.
  • nav / alert-\* — a milled channel with a plate riding in it, and recessed frames with a 3px rule down the left edge (telemetry blue, dusty green, ochre, oxide red), one step wider than the kit's own 2px seam so the rule reads as a warning lamp.
  • link — wet rust, underlined on hover: the same ink family as the action, because a link is an action even when it cannot be a fill.

Do's and Don'ts

  • Do keep the amber for fills. Set accent text in {colors.primary-text} (#5e2b03) — the fill is 1.83:1 as a label and no weight rescues it.
  • Do use the ground grade of the texture anywhere type sits, and the material grade only where it does not. That split is the kit.
  • Do express depth as a hard offset plate. If something needs a blur to be legible, it is the wrong element.
  • Do keep radii at 2–5px, including badges. The 3px "pill" is a specification, not an oversight.
  • Do let the shadow be longer than the object — that is what a low sun does.
  • Do keep the blue-grey for shadow, telemetry and focus. It is the only cool note.
  • Don't add a third saturated colour, a glow, a starfield, a violet, or anything that could be called cosmic. This is the ground, and the sky is out of frame.
  • Don't put white on the amber plate. It takes dust-black (#191410).
  • Don't blur anything: --blur is none, and a soft shadow would undo the entire premise.
  • Don't lighten the ground to "calm it down". The inks are solved against the darkest stop and the texture's worst composite; raising the ground past #ded9cd starts costing margins that were paid for.
