Frutiger Aero

Overview

Frutiger Aero is the 2004–2013 consumer-tech register: sky above, grass below, and translucent glass over both, with bubbles and water droplets in the air. It is bright and friendly rather than severe — the ground is a real gradient scene, the panels are genuinely see-through, and the light comes from a single source at the upper left.

This kit is the library's deliberate exception to its own non-smooth rule. Every other kit here is required to shut five doors against smoothness — square corners, zero-blur shadows, hard-stopped gradients, upscaled artwork, no blur. Frutiger Aero is smoothness: the gradients ramp, the shadows are soft and cast, --blur is real and load-bearing on every card, and there is a light bloom in the masthead. The one line that is not negotiable is legibility, and that is the kit's trade: gloss costs contrast headroom. A specular band lifts the luminance of a panel's top edge; a bright blue sky is a mid-luminance ground; a white label on a glossy control is the pair that fails first. Every ink here is solved against the darkest ground stop, and the working is in README.md.

What makes it read as glass rather than as fog is one structural decision: the specular band has a hard lower edge. A soft blur with no highlight is fog; a blurred panel with a crisp white band across its top is glass. The band is deliberate and pre-loadable ({colors.glass-band} over {colors.surface}), and it is capped at 13px on content panels — see Elevation & Depth. Measured on the rendered card, the band is a strip ~20 units whiter in red than the body below it, ending in a hard cut of ~15 units across a single pixel.

Colors

  • primary — {colors.primary} azure. The one action colour, and it is deliberately deeper than the register's poster blue: a bright sky blue (#2196f3) gives a white label 3.10:1 and cannot carry text. {colors.primary} measures 5.45:1.
  • primary-ink — {colors.primary-ink} the accent as text (links, the eyebrow, active nav and tab, badge labels, secondary-button labels, inline code, table code). The fill colour is only 3.89:1 as a small label on the sky, so text ships separately at 5.52:1.
  • secondary — {colors.secondary} grass green. Gradients, media, charts and the green end of the ramp. It is never a text colour.
  • tertiary — {colors.tertiary} the sky at the top of the horizon panel, and the solid stand-in for the media surface.
  • neutral — {colors.neutral} the ground's white middle, and the colour to use when a surface genuinely cannot be translucent (print, email, canvas). See also {colors.surface-solid}.
  • The ramp is addressable stop by stop: {colors.sky}, {colors.sky-2}, {colors.air}, {colors.grass}, {colors.grass-2}, with {colors.grass-deep} for the deepest green and {colors.horizon-spec} for the specular line at the horizon.
  • Surfaces are transparent by design. {colors.surface} and {colors.surface-2} are rgba() white. Never substitute a hex — the translucency is the kit, and --blur is applied on top of it.
  • Lines are cool, not white. {colors.border} is a soft sky-blue hairline, because a white hairline is invisible on this ground. The bright edge of a pane is a separate job and is done inside the shadow ({colors.glass-band}-adjacent insets), not by the border.
  • Status colours ({colors.ok}, {colors.warn}, {colors.danger}, {colors.info}) are all solved against the page ground, not just against a white card: the Forms row sets labels and hints straight on the sky with no panel behind them.
  • {colors.fill-top}, {colors.fill-low} and {colors.fill-foot} are the progress/avatar fill. They exist as separate values because .avatar typesets a white monogram on them: the upper band is capped at 10% white over {colors.fill-top} (4.71:1) and the lower at 14% over {colors.fill-low} (5.99:1). A brighter, prettier band fails the avatar.

Typography

Hind for display and body, IBM Plex Mono for captions and token labels.

The register's typeface is Frutiger — a commercial Linotype licence, and this repository is public, so the kit ships a Frutiger-adjacent free humanist sans instead. Hind is humanist with open apertures and an even colour, which is the part of Frutiger that carries the era's feel; "Frutiger" appears in the stack as a system fallback only, never as a Google Fonts request.

The register leans towards a light-to-regular display weight. The shared lab fixes display weight at 700 for .t-display and .masthead h1 and exposes no weight hook, so this kit's light display voice is expressed where it can be: near-neutral tracking ({typography.display.letterSpacing}, not a modern-SaaS -0.02em) and generous radii. Body copy is {typography.body.fontSize} / 1.6 — on a saturated ground, leading is what keeps paragraphs readable. Labels run in mono uppercase at {typography.label.letterSpacing} and never below 12px.

Layout

The ground is a scene, and it should always be visible: the sky at the top of a page, white through the middle, the green register at the foot. Panels float on it rather than covering it — keep 16–28px between surfaces so the sky shows between them, and never fill a page with an opaque surface.

The full ground walk is expressed in pixel stops (380/760/1150/1950/2550/3300px), not percentages: the body's gradient box is the whole document, so a 0% → 100% ramp smears the sky over several thousand pixels and the first viewport reads as flat near-white.

Elevation & Depth

Three translucent layers, distinguished by opacity and shadow rather than by colour:

1. ground — the gradient scene on --bg, plus a drawn bubble field; 2. {colors.surface} — 34% white plus the specular band, with backdrop-filter: blur(16px) saturate(155%); 3. {colors.surface-2} — 44% white, a soft glass with no band, for everything nested: inputs, badges, alerts, code, the switch track, the tile base.

The blur is load-bearing — templates/lab.css applies var(--blur) to every .card, and a kit that sets --blur: none gets no frost at all.

Why 34% and not 46%. The first build of this kit used 46% white plus a brightness(1.04) term in --blur, and the band came back invisible: measured on the rendered card, the panel composited to L 0.989 and the band's plateau to L 0.991 — a step of 0.002. A specular highlight needs headroom above the surface it sits on, and a near-white panel has none. The brightness() term was removed and the base brought down, which is why the bloom now lives only in --bg and --wash, where it lightens the ground before the glass is composited over it. {colors.surface-2} is deliberately denser than {colors.surface} — nested surfaces should be, and it is what makes an input read as a pane inside a pane.

One consequence of the shared lab worth knowing: .card-elevated reads --surface-2, not --surface, so the raised card in the Cards row carries the denser nested glass and no specular band. Giving both card tiers the band would mean putting one on --surface-2, which also backs .input, .badge, .alert and pre.code — where a hard edge at 46% of a 41px field cuts through the text inside it. The band therefore appears on the base card, the nav, the masthead lip and the media panel, and the raised panel is the smooth-glass one.

The band, and why it is 13px. The specular band is the identity, and it has a hard lower edge: 52% white at 12px straight to 2% at 13px. On the material sample — .card-media and the horizon token — the band is a hard-edged sweep across the top third (22px, which is 23% of the 96px panel and 31% of the 72px tile), where nothing is ever typeset. On content panels it is capped at 13px, and that is measured rather than timid: the lab puts the first line of type 18px below a card's top edge and around 17px below a nav item's, so a percentage band's edge lands inside .card-body on the Cards row — a hard white line across a paragraph. The kit keeps the hard edge and moves the edge, rather than softening the edge.

{colors.surface-2} has no band at all, on purpose: the same token backs .input, .badge, .alert and pre.code, and a hard edge inside a 41px field cuts straight through the text inside it.

Shadows are soft and cast (--shadow-1 / --shadow-2, each two cast layers plus an inset white top highlight and a faint bottom bounce). --glow is the primary button's own lift: a cast shadow plus an inner gloss.

Shapes

{rounded.sm} 10px, {rounded.md} 16px, {rounded.lg} 22px, pills at 999px, --cut: 0px. Generous and consistent — the panel and the ground it floats on should feel cut from the same mould. No chamfer: --clip is not declared, so --frame-ring is inert, and the kit's geometry is curves only. Native check/radio keep the browser's own rendering with a --check-radius of 6px, so a radio stays a circle while the box becomes a rounded square.

Components

  • button-primary — {colors.primary} with a white label, a cast shadow and an inner gloss; hover deepens to {colors.primary-hover} rather than brightening (a gloss darkens). Pressed, it travels 1px down as the bevel collapses.
  • button-secondary — the glass itself as a control: 34% white with the same specular band and a {colors.border} rim, labelled in {colors.primary-ink}.
  • card — 34% white, the 13px specular band, a {colors.border} rim, --shadow-1, and {colors.text} inside because the panel is lighter than the sky behind it.
  • card-media — the material sample: sky over grass with a hard specular line at the horizon, under a top-third gloss sweep. No text is ever set on it.
  • input — soft glass at {colors.surface-2} with a recessed --input-inset and a bright lower bevel; focus switches the rim to {colors.primary} and adds a soft ring.
  • badge — pill, mono, uppercase; badge-accent is the {colors.badge-tint} variant with a {colors.primary-ink} label (5.33:1 composited, measured).
  • nav / nav-item-active — a glass rail with a specular plate under the current item.
  • progress / avatar — the hard-banded blue-to-green fill, gloss through the middle. The avatar's monogram is {colors.on-primary} and is the reason the fill is not brighter.
  • modal-scrim — {colors.overlay}, a deep marine blue rather than neutral black.

Do's and Don'ts

Do

  • Let the sky and the grass show between panels — the ground is half the kit.
  • Keep the specular band's lower edge hard. If it fades, the glass becomes fog.
  • Grade any new ink against the darkest ground stop ({colors.sky}) and against the gloss band at full alpha. A light kit binds on its most saturated ground, not its palest.
  • Put vivid, saturated blue where nothing is typeset: media panels, the drawn motifs, the progress fill. The ink stays dark.

Don't

  • Don't set --blur: none or drop backdrop-filter — the kit becomes a flat pastel page.
  • Don't raise surface opacity much above 50%: frosted turns to painted, the sky stops reading through the panel, and the specular band loses the headroom it needs to be seen.
  • Don't put a white label on glass. White belongs on {colors.primary} and on the fill.
  • Don't add a second light source. One highlight per sphere, upper left, always.
  • Don't use {colors.primary} as a large flat fill, and never as a text colour — that is what {colors.primary-ink} is for.
